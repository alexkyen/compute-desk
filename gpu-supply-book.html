Why the sweet spot existsThe model & its limits

The committed layer is paid for every hour whether used or not. Demand above it is served at the elastic rate; unused committed capacity is paid idle. Import a single demand column or place demand in the final column after a timestamp. One optional header row and blank lines are allowed. Missing, invalid, or negative demand values stop the import; earlier columns are never substituted. Commitments use whole GPUs, and imported scenarios are optimized against the observed distribution.

commit a GPU ⇔ its expected utilization ≥ committed ÷ elastic price

The cost curve often has a broad minimum. Sweep K from zero to the peak and watch modeled cost fall, bottom out, and rise. Sizing error may be less important than duration, delivery, concentration, and counterparty exposure; this tool does not price those risks.

Volatility lowers the committed layer. Holding rates constant, a higher peak-to-average ratio places more demand in low-frequency intervals and moves the modeled optimum down.

A larger committed-rate discount raises the committed layer. Breakeven utilization equals committed price divided by elastic price, so lower committed pricing makes less frequently used capacity economical in the model.

Limitations. The tool assumes the observed distribution repeats, elastic capacity is always available, rates are flat, and one GPU-hour is interchangeable with another. It omits growth, minimum billing increments, placement, performance, outages, and contract optionality.
