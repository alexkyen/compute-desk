Section 01 of the guide

What you rent.

A GPU-hour buys a slice of a much larger machine. Memory decides what fits, the connections decide what can work together, and power and cooling decide where it can run at all.

The short version

  • Memory decides what fits. At 16-bit precision, a 70-billion-parameter model needs about 140 GB for its weights alone: more than an H100’s 80 GB, and barely inside an H200’s 141 GB.
  • Newer GPUs win on different measures. Memory, memory speed, compute, power, and how many GPUs connect directly each grew at a different rate, so rank them by what your work runs out of first.
  • At rack scale, the building becomes part of the product. An NVL72 joins 72 GPUs into one NVLink domain and draws about 120 kW, which needs liquid cooling the facility has to deliver.

01Will the model fit?

Every parameter takes up memory: 2 bytes at 16-bit precision, 1 byte at 8-bit, half a byte at 4-bit. Multiply by the model size and you have the floor for its weights. If they do not fit on one GPU, the model has to be split across several, and those GPUs have to talk to each other on every step.

02How the GPUs compare

Each generation improves some measures far more than others. Pick the one your work runs out of first, then choose a GPU to read what it means for a buyer.

03Inside the rack

The GB200 NVL72 puts 72 GPUs in one rack: eighteen compute trays of four GPUs each, with nine switch trays in the middle that connect every GPU to every other over NVLink. Turn it, then take it apart one chapter at a time, down to a single chip.

Assumptions, limits, and sources

Specifications are not benchmarks. Compute figures use different precisions and may include sparsity, so they cannot be turned straight into a speedup or a price premium. Benchmark the model you will run, on the serving or training stack you will use.

The NVLink domain ends at the rack. Inside it, every GPU can reach every other at high bandwidth while keeping its own memory. Traffic that leaves the rack moves to the scale-out network, which is bought and designed separately; section 02 models that boundary.

Power is the binding constraint. Roughly 120 kW in one rack is why liquid cooling is required, and why the scarce thing shifted from chips to facilities that can run them. Two suppliers quoting the same GPU can differ entirely in whether their building can hold this rack.

The rack can be what you buy, but it is not the only unit. Providers sell racks, servers, instances, or capacity blocks. Ask which physical assets, failure domains, and acceptance tests correspond to the unit in your quote.

Roadmaps belong in the contract. A multi-year term can overlap one or more launches. Write refresh, substitution, and rate-review rights into it rather than treating term length alone as savings.
